# HR-Analytics- Boosting-Retention-with-Data-Insights-at-Adecco-India-Using-Excel
1. Project Title / Headline -
   Boosting Retention with Data Insights at Adecco India

2. Short Description / Purpose -
  Adecco India is a medium-sized technology company specializing in software development. It has a diverse workforce spread across various departments, including 
  engineering, marketing, sales, and customer support. Recently, Adecco India has observed an increase in employee turnover, especially among junior-level 
  employees, which is concerning for the management. The company is committed to understanding the factors influencing employee attrition and job satisfaction to 
  develop strategies for improving employee engagement and retention.

3. What is the problem?
Adecco India is experiencing high employee turnover, particularly in the sales department among junior-level employees. This turnover is costly and disrupts team dynamics, leading to decreased productivity and morale.

Why is it important to solve it?
High attrition rates can lead to significant costs associated with recruitment, onboarding, and training of new employees. It also affects the company's ability to maintain consistent performance and achieve long-term goals. Improving employee retention will enhance productivity, reduce costs, and foster a more stable and motivated workforce.

4. Tech Stack - Excel

5.  Features / Highlights -Built an HR analytics dashboard to help Adecco India identify the key drivers of employee attrition and improve retention through data-backed decisions. Tracks attrition rate, job satisfaction, performance ratings, and tenure across departments, income bands, and tenure milestones.

    Walkthrough of Key Visuals - 
    *KPI Scorecards — Attrition Rate (16.12%), Job Satisfaction Score (2.73), Performance Ratings (3.15), Tenure (7.01 yrs) — gives leadership a one-glance health check
    * Average Monthly Income of Employees Who Left (Donut/Pie) — Shows ₹4,787.09 as the average income of departed employees, flagging if attrition skews toward lower-paid staff
    * Years at Company vs. Employee Departures (Line Chart) — Tracks attrition against tenure, showing departures spike sharply in the first 1-6 years and drop off after
    * Overtime vs Attrition (Donut Chart) — Splits employees by overtime status (1054 No vs 416 Yes), highlighting overtime as a potential attrition driver
    * Years in Current Role vs Attrition (Line Chart) — Shows attrition peaks around year 2-3 in a role, suggesting a stagnation/promotion-timing risk window
    * Stock Option Level vs Attrition Rate (Bar Chart) — Compares attrition % across 4 stock option levels (0-3), showing level 0 has the highest attrition (24.41%)
    * Attrition by Income (Bar Chart) — Breaks down attrition counts by income band: Low (113), Medium (77), High (47) — lower income correlates with higher attrition
    * Department-wise Attrition (Bar Chart) — R&D (56.12%) and Sales (38.82%) show far higher attrition than HR (5.06%), pointing to department-specific retention issues
